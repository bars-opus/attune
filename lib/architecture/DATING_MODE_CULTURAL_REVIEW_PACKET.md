# Dating Mode Cultural Review Packet

**Audience:** Ghanaian / West African cultural reviewer  
**Purpose:** check whether Dating Mode language, defaults, prompts, and assumptions fit the target context without forcing a Western dating-app model

## 1. Review scope

This review is not asking whether the feature is technically correct. It is asking whether the current product language and assumptions are culturally appropriate for Ghanaian and West African users.

## 2. Current implemented choices

### Defaults and geography

- Dating profile city defaults begin with Ghanaian cities.
- The UI uses city/region rather than exact distance.

### Product posture

- No swiping, admirer queues, urgency timers, streaks, or gamified revelation.
- Dating is framed as curated introductions, not a browsable marketplace.
- Copy emphasizes modesty, curiosity, privacy, and self-reported signals.

### Guided-date prompts

Current prompts include:

- "What has felt grounding for you lately?"
- "What kind of encouragement actually lands for you?"
- "What does a genuinely restful weekend look like?"
- "What are you hoping to learn about someone as you date?"

## 3. Cultural review questions

Record `approve`, `approve with changes`, or `reject` for each.

### Language and assumptions

- [ ] The product avoids overly Western assumptions about dating pace, exclusivity, or self-disclosure.
- [ ] The phrase "Dating Mode" is acceptable for the intended launch audience.
- [ ] "Alignment preview" is understandable and not misleading in this context.
- [ ] The prompts sound socially and emotionally natural for Ghanaian users.

### Social context

- [ ] The current flow does not ignore the role of family, communal expectations, respectability, or social obligations.
- [ ] The values layer should remain individual-only in v1.
- [ ] A future reviewed communal/family-context values layer would be useful.

### Safety and support

- [ ] Safety wording feels usable and culturally appropriate.
- [ ] Ghana-focused support resources and guidance are sufficient for initial release planning.

## 4. Required feedback if not approved

- Which phrases sound imported, awkward, too individualistic, too vague, or too direct?
- Which profile or prompt fields should be added, removed, or reworded?
- Should any guidance explicitly acknowledge family or communal context?
- Are there safer or more culturally natural terms than "Dating Mode" or "Alignment preview"?
